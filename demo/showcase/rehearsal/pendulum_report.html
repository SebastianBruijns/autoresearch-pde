Summary. The data (4 trajectories of angle theta and angular velocity omega over 15 s) are described by a damped nonlinear pendulum: dθ/dt = ω and dω/dt = −9.81·sin θ − 0.1·ω, meaning a gravity-like restoring torque proportional to sin θ plus a weak linear (viscous) damping.

Physical interpretation. θ' = ω is the kinematic definition of angular velocity. The −9.81·sin θ term is the gravitational restoring torque per unit inertia, g/L, consistent with g ≈ 9.81 m/s² and an effective length of about 1 m if units are SI; this is a guess, since the data card gives no units. The −0.1·ω term is weak viscous damping (for example air drag or pivot friction) with a decay timescale of roughly 10–20 s, which is comparable to the 15 s record length.

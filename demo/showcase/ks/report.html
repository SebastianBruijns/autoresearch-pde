Summary. The data come from a one-dimensional field u(x,t) that develops a front which breaks up into spatiotemporal cellular chaos, and the discovered equation is a rescaled Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation: u_t = −0.569·u·u_x − 1.184·u_xx − 1.998·u_xxxx, combining nonlinear advection, a destabilising negative diffusion and stabilising fourth-order hyperdiffusion.

Physical interpretation. The −u·u_x term is Burgers-type nonlinear self-advection, which transfers energy between scales and steepens gradients. The −u_xx term is negative (anti-)diffusion that amplifies long-wavelength perturbations. The −u_xxxx term is hyperdiffusion that damps short wavelengths. Their balance selects a preferred cell size: the fastest-growing wavenumber is k* = √(1.184/(2·1.998)) ≈ 0.54, with marginal wavenumber √(1.184/1.998) ≈ 0.77. Together the three terms produce the chaotic cellular pattern typical of flame fronts, thin falling films or phase turbulence; the coefficients are simply a rescaling of the canonical KS form.

Caveats
  • All results come from a single trajectory. The coefficients are precise for this run, but generalisation to other initial conditions or amplitudes has not been tested.
  • Source terms (1, u, u²) were excluded a priori on the basis of mean conservation and were never re-tested as candidates in the missing-term analysis.
  • Rollout metrics conflict. The tool-reported valid time is only ≈6.3 time units (about 11% of the span), while the assessment reports held-out agreement for the full horizon. For a chaotic system, pointwise trajectory divergence is expected and is not by itself evidence of model error.
  • The skeleton refit returned wildly different coefficients with the same valid-time score. This suggests the valid-time metric is insensitive or the optimiser failed, so valid time should not be used to rank coefficient sets here.
  • Domain length and boundary conditions (presumably periodic) are not documented in the data card. Grid units for x and t are also unstated.

Intuition (pre-analysis, before any fitting)

  • [low] u is statistically symmetric under u -> -u: nonlinearities are likely odd (u^3) or of advective type; quadratic sources are unlikely

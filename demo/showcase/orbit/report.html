Summary. The data are a single 3-D trajectory of a test particle orbiting an oblate central body, and the discovered equation is Newtonian point-mass gravity (μ = 1) plus the axisymmetric J2 oblateness correction: a = −r/|r|³ · [1 + 0.75/|r|² · (1 − 5z²/|r|²)] in x and y, with (3 − 5z²/|r|²) in z.

Physical interpretation. The −r/|r|³ term is Newtonian gravity of the central mass, with GM normalised to 1. The 0.75/|r|² bracket term is the J2 quadrupole perturbation from the body's equatorial bulge, with strength (3/2)·J2·R² = 0.75. Its z²-dependence pulls the orbit toward the equatorial plane and makes it non-Keplerian, so the orbital plane precesses about the z-axis and the periapsis rotates. The different constant in the z-equation (3 instead of 1) is exactly the gradient of the J2 potential −(μ J2 R²/2r³)(3z²/r² − 1), so the force is conservative.

Caveats
  • Only one trajectory was available, covering radii ≈ 1.1–2.5. Higher harmonics (J3, J4) that matter mostly at small radius are constrained only within this range.
  • Derivative-based bootstrap CIs on the J2 coefficients (e.g. −0.731 [−0.740, −0.722] vs submitted −0.75) technically exclude the submitted values. This is consistent with smoothing bias. The trajectory-shooting fit (0.7501 ± 0.0001) is the more trustworthy estimate.
  • Long-horizon pointwise prediction is limited. The held-out rollout stays within 30% error for only a small fraction of the horizon, and the inter-model predictability horizon is ≈ 7.6 time units, roughly half an orbit. Tiny errors in μ or k accumulate as orbital phase drift, even though the orbit shape is reproduced well.
  • A borderline vx ∝ vy³ term (ΔBIC −3.2) was flagged. It is velocity-dependent, asymmetric, and has no physical basis in gravitational dynamics, so it is most likely a noise artifact.
  • Assessment confidence is rated medium. The main reason is single-trajectory coverage, not fit quality.
